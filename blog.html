Practical Website Tips

Short, useful guidance for small businesses that want their website to explain their services clearly and make it easier for customers to get in touch.

1. Make the Homepage Answer the Basics

A visitor should be able to understand what you do without hunting through several pages.

  • Describe the service in plain language.
  • Identify the town or service area, if relevant.
  • Show business hours or response expectations where useful.
  • Give visitors one clear next step, such as requesting a quote.

2. Make Contact Simple

A form works best when it asks only for information needed to respond.

  • Use visible labels for each field.
  • Ask for a name, email, and project message when those are enough to begin.
  • Explain how the business will respond.
  • Test the form and confirm where submissions are delivered before promoting it.

3. Choose Booking Tools Carefully

An appointment button can link to an external scheduler. Before adding one, check the customer experience and ongoing costs.

  • Confirm services, availability, time zone, cancellation rules, and notifications.
  • Identify any subscription, text-message, or transaction fees.
  • Make sure the business owner can maintain the schedule.

4. Plan Workflow Automation Before Buying Tools

Start with a specific repetitive task rather than choosing a tool first.

  • Write down the task and what triggers it.
  • Identify the current apps or documents involved.
  • Define what should happen if the automation fails or needs human review.
  • Compare setup costs, recurring fees, and privacy implications.

5. Review Accessibility and Mobile Use

Check the website on a phone and with keyboard navigation, not only on a desktop.

  • Use descriptive page titles and headings.
  • Keep text readable and button labels clear.
  • Make sure forms have labels and usable error messages.
  • Provide important contact information in written form.

6. Keep Prices and Project Scope Clear

Before paid website work starts, agree on the deliverables and what is not included.

  • List pages, features, content responsibilities, and revision limits.
  • Confirm who pays for hosting, domains, email, booking tools, and subscriptions.
  • Agree on the timeline and handoff plan in writing.
